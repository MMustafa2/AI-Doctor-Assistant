# Treatment guide for the Pakistan doctor assistant (REFERENCE DRAFT)

Note: general first-line options written from common clinical practice. A licensed doctor or pharmacist must verify this file (and Pakistan's national guidelines) before real use. Doses are for adults unless stated. Children are dosed by weight by a doctor. Antibiotics, antimalarials and ondansetron need a doctor's confirmation.

## Fever (viral or unknown cause)
Medicine: paracetamol 500-1000 mg every 6-8 hours as needed (maximum 3 g per day). Drink plenty of fluids, rest, tepid sponging. Antibiotics are not needed for viral fever. Never give aspirin to children. See a doctor if fever lasts more than 3 days, is above 39.5 C, or comes with rash, stiff neck, confusion or breathing trouble.

## Dengue
Medicine: paracetamol 500 mg every 6-8 hours only for fever and pain (maximum 3 g per day). Oral fluids 2.5-3 litres per day (ORS, soups, coconut water), rest. AVOID aspirin, ibuprofen, diclofenac and all NSAIDs; also no steroids or antibiotics unless a doctor decides. Blood test (CBC with platelets and hematocrit) every 24-48 hours from day 3. Go to hospital for bleeding, severe stomach pain, repeated vomiting, drowsiness, cold hands and feet, or weakness when the fever falls (day 3-5, critical phase).

## Malaria
Do NOT take antimalarial medicine without a positive test (blood smear or rapid test). Paracetamol for fever meanwhile. Treatment depends on the parasite: vivax is treated with chloroquine plus primaquine (G6PD test first); falciparum with an artemisinin combination such as artemether-lumefantrine. A doctor prescribes it. Severe malaria (confusion, fits, very weak, jaundice) is an emergency.

## Typhoid (enteric fever)
Confirm with blood culture (Widal alone is unreliable). Paracetamol for fever, fluids, soft diet. Antibiotic is chosen by the doctor: azithromycin 500 mg once daily for 7 days is a common oral choice, but many typhoid strains in Pakistan are resistant, so culture-based treatment is best. Hospital if persistent vomiting, severe stomach pain, confusion or bleeding.

## Diarrhea and gastroenteritis
Medicine: ORS (one sachet in 1 litre of clean water): adults 200-400 ml after each loose stool, sip often. Children: zinc 20 mg daily for 10-14 days (10 mg if under 6 months) plus ORS. Continue normal food, avoid sugary drinks. Antibiotics are not routinely needed. Adults without fever or blood in stool may take loperamide 4 mg first, then 2 mg after each loose stool (maximum 8 mg per day). See a doctor for blood in stool, high fever, more than 3 days, dehydration (very thirsty, no urine, dizziness), infants and elderly.

## Vomiting and nausea
Small sips of ORS, rest, bland diet. If persistent, ondansetron 4 mg up to three times daily (prescription medicine, doctor confirms). See a doctor if more than 24 hours, blood in vomit, severe pain, or fluids cannot be kept down.

## Acidity, gastritis and heartburn
Medicine: antacid (aluminium and magnesium hydroxide) 10 ml after meals and at bedtime, or omeprazole 20 mg once daily 30 minutes before breakfast for 14 days. Avoid spicy and fried food, tea and coffee on an empty stomach, NSAID painkillers and smoking. Eat small meals and do not lie down right after eating. See a doctor for black stool, vomiting blood, weight loss, trouble swallowing, or pain for more than 2 weeks.

## Common cold and flu-like illness (viral)
Medicine: paracetamol for fever and aches, saline nasal drops or steam, warm fluids, honey in warm water (not under 1 year). For a runny nose, cetirizine 10 mg at night (causes sleepiness) or chlorpheniramine 4 mg every 6-8 hours. No antibiotics for colds. Oseltamivir 75 mg twice daily for 5 days is only for suspected influenza in high-risk patients within 48 hours (doctor decides). See a doctor for breathlessness, fever over 3 days or chest pain.

## Allergic rhinitis, allergy and itching
Medicine: cetirizine 10 mg once daily or loratadine 10 mg once daily; avoid dust, smoke and other triggers. Emergency if face or lip swelling or breathing trouble.

## Cough
Dry cough: honey with warm water, dextromethorphan 10-20 mg every 6-8 hours (adults). Cough with phlegm: ambroxol 30 mg three times daily, steam, plenty of fluids. Antibiotics are not needed for most coughs. See a doctor if cough lasts more than 3 weeks, or there is blood in sputum, breathlessness, weight loss or night sweats (TB test).

## Sore throat
Mostly viral: warm salt-water gargle, lozenges, paracetamol, fluids. If bacterial (strep throat: fever, white spots on tonsils, no cough, swollen neck glands) the doctor decides: amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for 10 days is first choice if there is no penicillin allergy; azithromycin if allergic.

## Urinary tract infection (UTI)
Drink plenty of water. Do a urine test (routine and culture) before an antibiotic. The doctor chooses the antibiotic from the report because resistance is common in Pakistan. Nitrofurantoin 100 mg twice daily for 5 days is a common option for simple bladder infection in women (avoid in late pregnancy and kidney disease). Urgent doctor visit for fever, flank pain, vomiting, pregnancy, men and children.

## Headache and migraine
Medicine: paracetamol 500-1000 mg, or ibuprofen 400 mg after food. Rest in a dark quiet room, fluids, sleep. Limit painkillers to 2-3 days per week to avoid rebound headache. Emergency or doctor for sudden worst headache, fever with stiff neck, weakness, vision change, or headache after a head injury.

## Muscle pain, sprain and minor injury (no deformity)
Rest, ice for 15 minutes every 3-4 hours for the first 48 hours, compression, elevation. Medicine: ibuprofen 400 mg three times daily after food for 3-5 days (not with stomach ulcer, kidney disease, NSAID-triggered asthma, pregnancy or dengue), or paracetamol; diclofenac gel on the skin 2-3 times daily. Get an X-ray if the patient cannot bear weight, there is deformity, severe swelling or numbness.

## Asthma and wheeze
Medicine: salbutamol inhaler 100 mcg, 1-2 puffs when needed; avoid dust, smoke and other triggers. A regular controller inhaler is started by a doctor. Emergency if the patient cannot speak full sentences, has blue lips, or gets no relief from the inhaler.

## Pneumonia and chest infection
Needs a doctor and a chest X-ray. For mild pneumonia in adults the usual first choice is amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for 5 days (doctor decides). Fluids, rest, paracetamol. Hospital for breathlessness, low oxygen, confusion, elderly patients and children.

## Scabies
Medicine: permethrin 5% cream: apply from neck to toes after a bath, wash off after 8-12 hours, repeat after 7 days. Treat all household contacts, wash bedding and clothes in hot water. Cetirizine 10 mg at night for itching. Itch can continue for 2-4 weeks.

## Chickenpox
Paracetamol for fever (never aspirin or ibuprofen), calamine lotion, trim nails, cetirizine at night for itch. Acyclovir is for adults, pregnant women, newborns and weak immunity, by a doctor. Stay home until all blisters have crusted.

## Anemia (tiredness, pale skin)
Needs CBC and ferritin tests. Iron: ferrous sulfate 200 mg (65 mg iron) once or twice daily with vitamin C for about 3 months (doctor confirms). Dark stool is normal; it may cause constipation. Eat iron-rich food. Find the cause (diet, heavy periods, worms, bleeding).

## Constipation
Water, fibre, isabgol (psyllium husk) 1-2 tablespoons in a glass of water at night, or lactulose 15 ml at night. See a doctor for blood in stool, weight loss or severe pain.

## Heat exhaustion and heat stroke
Heat exhaustion: move to shade, cool the body with wet cloths, drink ORS and water, rest. Heat stroke (confusion, no sweating, temperature above 40 C, fainting) is an EMERGENCY: call 1122, cool with wet cloths and ice packs while waiting. No medicine; paracetamol does not help in heat stroke.

## Hepatitis A and jaundice
Rest, fluids, light food, no alcohol; paracetamol maximum 2 g per day only; avoid unnecessary medicines. Needs a liver function test. Hepatitis B and C need a specialist and tests.

## Tuberculosis (TB)
Cough for more than 2 weeks, night sweats, weight loss or blood in sputum: get a sputum test and chest X-ray at a government TB centre (treatment is free under the national TB program). Never self-treat.

## Diabetes and high blood pressure
Long-term conditions. Medicines (metformin, amlodipine and others) are started and adjusted by a doctor after tests. Advice: healthy diet, daily walking, less salt and sugar, regular checks. Emergency for very high sugar with vomiting or confusion, or high blood pressure with chest pain, severe headache or weakness.
